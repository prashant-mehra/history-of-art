Prehistoric art

~100,000 – 2000 BCE · Worldwide

"Prehistoric" means before written records. The art of this immense span is known only through what survived: paint on cave walls, carved bone and ivory, fired clay and stone monuments. We do not know what its makers called it, or what it meant to them. Everything we say about its purpose is inference.

The Stone Age is divided by tools and ways of life into three broad periods. They overlap, and they began and ended at different times in different places.

Old Stone Age

Paleolithic

Hunter-gatherers; cave painting, portable figurines.

 Middle Stone Age

Mesolithic

Warming climate; narrative rock art, early monuments.

 New Stone Age

Neolithic

Farming; pottery, settled villages, megaliths.

Questions this art raises

  • Why make it? Ritual, storytelling, hunting magic, marking territory and social identity have all been proposed. None is proven.
  • Who made it? Mostly unknown. Studies of hand stencils suggest both men and women took part.
  • How do we date it? Radiocarbon dating of charcoal and pigment, and uranium-series dating of mineral crusts over paintings. Dates are regularly revised.
