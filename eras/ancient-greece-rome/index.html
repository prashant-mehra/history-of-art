Ancient Greece and Rome

~900 BCE–476 CE

Greek artists developed an ideal of the human body and a language of architecture, which Rome adopted, adapted and spread across its empire.

Greece: from Archaic to Hellenistic

Archaic statues (~700–480 BCE) stand stiffly with a fixed smile. In the Classical period (480–323 BCE) sculptors mastered natural poses with weight and balance (contrapposto). The Hellenistic age (323–31 BCE) turned to drama, emotion and the everyday.

Architecture

The Doric, Ionic and Corinthian orders defined temple design. The Parthenon is the best-known example.

Rome

Romans were great portraitists, who valued realism in faces, and great builders, developing the arch, vault, dome and concrete. Aqueducts, the Colosseum and the Pantheon show this engineering.

Painting and mosaic

Wall paintings at Pompeii and floor mosaics survive where much Greek painting does not.

Legacy

Renaissance, Baroque and Neoclassical artists repeatedly returned to Greece and Rome as models.
