Contemporary art

~1970–today

Contemporary art has no single style. It is global, mixed in media, and concerned with identity, politics and technology.

Concept over object

Conceptual art treats the idea as the work. Performance, video and installation are as central as painting.

Identity and politics

Feminist art, postcolonial critique, and work on race, gender, migration and the environment fill biennials and museums.

Globalization

Artists from Africa, Asia and Latin America became prominent through biennials such as Venice, Documenta and Gwangju.

Market and spectacle

Auction records, blockbuster shows and art fairs raised both the profile and the price of art.

Digital art

From video art to net art, generative work and NFTs, technology is both tool and subject. Debates continue over what lasts and what counts.

Street art

Graffiti and street art moved from walls to museums, with Basquiat, Banksy and others.

Movements and styles

~1960s–1970s

Minimalism

Stripped-down geometric forms and industrial materials. Associated with Donald Judd and Agnes Martin. Kusama's early work is often discussed alongside it.

~1960s onward

Conceptual art

The idea is more important than the finished object. Includes text pieces, performance and installations.

~1970s onward

Street art and graffiti

Art made in public space without permission, or in dialogue with it, from tags and murals to stencils.

~1960s onward

Digital art

Art made with or about computers: algorithmic, networked, generative and interactive work.
