Ancient Egypt

~3100–30 BCE

Egyptian art remained recognizable for nearly 3,000 years. It was made to serve religion and the afterlife, and it follows strict conventions.

Conventions

Figures were shown with head and legs in profile and the torso from the front, and the size of a figure showed status. Statues were stiff and balanced because they were homes for the spirit.

Old Kingdom (~2686–2181 BCE)

The pyramids at Giza and the Great Sphinx date to this time, along with calm, idealized statues of rulers.

New Kingdom (~1550–1070 BCE)

Rich tombs, such as that of Tutankhamun, and monumental temples at Karnak and Abu Simbel. The Amarna period under Akhenaten (~1353–1336 BCE) briefly introduced a more naturalistic, curving style, seen in the bust of Nefertiti.

Materials and legacy

Gold, faience, limestone, granite and painted wood. Hieroglyphs are themselves an art form, often used as decoration.
