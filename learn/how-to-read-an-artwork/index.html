How to read an artwork

You do not need prior knowledge. A practiced way of looking helps.

1. Look slowly

Spend a full minute before reading any label. What do you notice first? What is easy to miss?

2. Describe

Say what you see in plain words: subject, colors, lines, textures, scale. Describing comes before interpreting.

3. Ask how it was made

What is the medium? Is the surface smooth or built up? Material choices carry meaning, and techniques limit what is possible.

4. Place it

When and where was it made, and for whom? A painting for a church altar and one for a private collector do different jobs.

5. Interpret

What might it mean? Use evidence in the image first, then context. Several readings can be valid.

6. Compare

Put it next to another work: an earlier one it learns from, or a later one that answers it. Compare Botticelli's Venus with Manet's Olympia, for instance.

Try it on The Starry Night or Guernica.
