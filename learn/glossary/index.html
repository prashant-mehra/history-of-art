Glossary

Arabesque
Flowing, interlaced plant-like decoration, typical of Islamic art.
Ashlar
Finely cut, squared stone masonry.
Atelier
A workshop or studio where artists and assistants work.
Baroque
Style of the 1600s–early 1700s marked by drama and movement.
BCE / CE
Before Common Era / Common Era; equivalents of BC / AD.
Bitumen
Natural tar, used in Neolithic art to outline eyes and as an adhesive.
Buttress
A projecting support for a wall; flying buttresses arch away from it.
Chiaroscuro
Strong contrast of light and dark to create volume.
Chronology (relative vs absolute)
Relative dating orders things before/after; absolute dating gives calendar ages, e.g. radiocarbon.
Contrapposto
A pose with weight on one leg, giving a relaxed, lifelike S-curve.
Cuneiform
Wedge-shaped writing used in ancient Mesopotamia.
Engraving
Lines cut into a surface with a hard point.
Figurine
A small sculpture, usually of a human or animal.
Fresco
Painting on wet plaster.
Frieze
A horizontal band of sculpture or decoration.
Glaze
A thin transparent layer of paint, or a glassy coating on ceramics.
Hunter-gatherer
A way of life based on wild foods; all humans lived this way before farming.
Iconography
The study of the symbols and subjects in images.
Illuminated manuscript
A handwritten book decorated with paint and gold.
Impasto
Thick paint that stands out from the surface.
Linear perspective
A system for showing depth with lines converging at a vanishing point.
Medium
The material an artwork is made of.
Megalith
A large stone used in a prehistoric monument.
Mosaic
An image made of small pieces (tesserae) of stone or glass.
Ochre
Natural iron-oxide earth pigment, red to yellow; the main colour of early art.
Parietal art
Art on cave walls or rock surfaces, as opposed to portable objects.
Passage tomb
A burial chamber reached by a stone-lined passage, covered by a mound.
Patron
Someone who commissions or supports art.
Petroglyph
Image carved or pecked into rock. A painted image is a pictograph.
Plein air
Painting outdoors.
Portable art (mobiliary art)
Small art objects that can be carried.
Provenance
The ownership history of an artwork.
Radiocarbon dating
Measures the decay of carbon-14 in organic material (works to roughly 50,000 years).
Ready-made
An ordinary object selected and presented as art.
Relief
Sculpture projecting from a flat background.
Sfumato
Soft blending of tones without hard outlines, used by Leonardo.
Stele
An upright stone slab with inscriptions or carving.
Triptych
An artwork in three panels.
Uranium-series dating
Dates mineral crusts that formed over paintings, giving a minimum age.
Vanitas
A still life reminding us of mortality and the vanity of earthly things.
Venus figurine
Modern name for Paleolithic female statuettes; the name is a later invention.
Woodcut
A print made from a carved wooden block.
Ziggurat
A stepped temple tower of ancient Mesopotamia.
